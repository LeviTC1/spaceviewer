# SpaceViewer — the whole product (pass 2, 23 Nov 2026)

## What I found when I used it

* The new universe explorer felt like its own product, while the image archive behind it still looked
  like the old one: a sidebar, a seven-tab dashboard, and status text like "Panel opened" and
  "Opens on demand".
* There were three searches (the explorer, the archive and ⌘K), and each covered only part of the
  site.
* Every route between tools was a jump into another app with its own header:
  * the universe → *Images* → an archive search page;
  * an archive image → no way back to the object in 3D;
  * Solar Explorer and the classic SkyMap → their own headers again.
* The *Live Feed From Space* tab was broken: the embedded NASA stream now says "This video is private".
* Saved state was scattered across tabs: For You, the Gallery collection, Solar bookmarks and recent
  searches. Explorer views could be shared, but there was no way to keep one.

## The idea

**Every object is a place and a photograph.**

SpaceViewer is the one place where you can fly to Saturn *and* open the frame Cassini took of it, or
look at a Webb image *and* fly to where that light came from. Everything else follows from that:

* **One instrument, one bar, one search.** Every screen sits under the same bar:
  * Universe · Images · Your sky;
  * Tools for the specialist labs;
  * one search, "Find anything": places, photographs, moments, your saved things and commands.
* **The front door is the live universe.** Opening on a sunlit Earth that is already moving shows
  what SpaceViewer is better than any explanation. The first card offers three ways in:
  * *Show me something incredible*;
  * a *Moment* from history you can replay;
  * search.

  A returning visitor gets *Continue where you left off* instead.
* **Bridges everywhere:**
  * From a place: *Seen by telescopes*, a filmstrip of real archive photographs inside the object
    panel.
  * From a photograph: *Where is this?*, with the place, how long its light took to reach us
    (derived and labelled), and *Fly there*.
* **Time is a feature, not a setting.** *Moments* replay real events at their real date:
  * New Horizons at Pluto;
  * Cassini's last orbit;
  * Voyager 1 leaving the heliosphere;
  * Halley at perihelion;
  * the 2003 Mars opposition.
* **Things you keep.** *Your sky* holds:
  * saved views (exact object, moment and camera);
  * saved photographs;
  * Solar Explorer bookmarks;
  * recent places and searches;
  * recommendations.
* **Postcards.** Any view can leave as a PNG with its caption and a link that reopens it. That's the
  thing people send to a friend.
* **Honesty is visible.** Facts carry a small mark: *measured*, *derived* or *illustrative*.

## Identity

* True black and warm white ink, as in the explorer.
* Amber means action: it is the colour of sunlight. Pale cyan means selection or "you are here".
* Editorial serif (New York / Iowan, system fonts) for names, titles and stories. Tabular sans for
  instruments and numbers.
* Photographs go edge to edge. Chrome is glass that never hides the subject.
* On phones, the thumb drives the camera and time in the universe and navigation everywhere else
  (a bottom bar).
